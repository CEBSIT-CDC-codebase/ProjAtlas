Q: How many transcriptome-defined cell types were identified in the macaque claustrum through single-nucleus RNA sequencing?
A: A total of 48 transcriptome-defined cell types were identified.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Single-nucleus RNA sequencing of 227,750 macaque claustral cells identified 48 transcriptome-defined cell types.

Q: What percentage of claustral glutamatergic cells resemble deep-layer insular neurons?
A: 86.4% of claustral glutamatergic cells resemble deep-layer (L5/6) insular neurons.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Interestingly, the great majority (86.4%) of claustral glutamatergic cells resembled deep-layer (L5/6) insular cells.

Q: Where is the macaque claustrum located in relation to the insular cortex?
A: The macaque claustrum is a thin telencephalic structure situated below the insular cortex.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: The claustrum, a thin telencephalic structure situated below the insular cortex,1 is reciprocally connected to nearly all cortical areas.

Q: What type of neurons are NPY-expressing SST GABAergic cells in the claustrum considered to be?
A: They are considered long-range projecting GABAergic neurons.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Interestingly, the percentage of neuropeptide Y (NPY)-expressing GABA/SST cells, known as long-range projecting GABAergic neurons,44–46 was much higher in the claustrum than in all cortical areas (Figure 2F), suggesting more substantial inhibitory output of the claustrum.

Q: Which genes are mentioned as claustral marker genes that distinguish the macaque claustrum from surrounding structures?
A: SYNPR, NR4A2, and NTNG2 are examples of claustral marker genes that distinguish the claustrum from surrounding structures.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: In line with previous studies,52–57 we found many claustral marker genes (such as SYNPR, NR4A2, and NTNG2) distinguish claustrum from surrounding structures (Figure 3A).

Q: Injections in which five subcortical structures yielded a significant number of labeled claustral neurons?
A: Injections in the thalamus, putamen, caudate nucleus, hippocampus, and amygdala yielded significant numbers of labeled claustral neurons.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: We have also injected retrograde tracers at 15 sites in seven subcortical structures and found that injections in thalamus, putamen, caudate nucleus, hippocampus, and amygdala (Figure S4J) yielded a significant number of labeled claustral neurons, which were distributed with distinct injection site-dependent patterns in the claustrum (Figure 4I).

Q: In cross-species comparison, which glutamatergic subclass was predominant in macaque and marmoset but only a minority in mouse?
A: The GNB4 glutamatergic subclass was predominant in macaque and marmoset but represented only a minority (14.7%) in mouse.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: For glutamatergic neurons, the GNB4 subclass was predominant in macaque and marmoset but was only a minority (14.7%) in mouse (Figure 2H), where ZFPM2 subclass was more abundant.

Q: How many cells were included in the macaque claustrum snRNA-seq analysis after quality control?
A: The analysis included 227,750 cells after quality control.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: After quality control (STAR Methods), the snRNA-seq analysis included 227,750 cells, with an average of 4,664 molecular identifiers (MIDs) per cell and 1,884 detected genes per cell (Figure S1A).

Q: What are the names of the four projection-selective zones defined in the macaque claustrum?
A: The four projection-selective zones are named PSZ1, PSZ2, PSZ3, and PSZ4.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: We then pooled all the labeled cells belonging to the same cluster and defined four overall ‘projection-selective zones’ (PSZs, 1–4), illustrated as domains of different colors in Figure 4D.

Q: What developmental origin is suggested for the LAMP5/LHX6 claustral cell types that lack ADARB2 expression?
A: They are suggested to originate from the medial ganglionic eminence (MGE).
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: By contrast, primate-specific cell types LAMP5/LHX6/ADARB2/1 and LAMP5/LHX6/ADARB2/ 4 showed minimal ADARB2 expression, suggesting their medial ganglionic eminence (MGE) origin.

Q: How many subclasses and cell types were identified from the macaque claustrum snRNA-seq data?
A: The UMAP analysis identified 16 subclasses, which were further divided into a total of 48 putative cell types.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Our UMAP analysis identified three major cell classes (‘‘glutamatergic’’ [GLUT]: 122,006 cells; ‘‘GABAergic’’ [GABA]: 34,293 cells; ‘‘non-neuronal’’: 71,451 cells), which were further divided into 16 subclasses (Figure 1B), including 3 glutamatergic (marked by GNB4, RGS6, and ZFPM2), 8 GABAergic (LAMP5, VIP, RELN, PVALB, SST, PDE10A, LHX6, and UNC5B), and 5 non-neuronal subclasses, including astrocytes (SLC1A2), oligodendrocyte precursor cells (OPCs, PCDH15), oligodendrocytes (MOBP), microglia (C1QB), and endothelial cells (FLT1).
  - Evidence 2: The 16 subclasses could be further divided into 48 putative ‘‘cell types’’: 22 glutamatergic, 19 GABAergic, and 7 non-neuronal cell types (Figures 1C and S1D; Table S1).

Q: Does the macaque claustrum exhibit a lateralized pattern of connectivity?
A: Yes, claustral connectivity has a strong ipsilateral bias; although many cortical areas receive bilateral projections, the projection strength from the contralateral claustrum is much weaker than from the ipsilateral claustrum.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Joint analysis of whole-brain connectivity and single-cell spatial transcriptome showed that these four zones containing distinct compositions of glutamatergic (but not GABAergic) cell types preferentially connected to specific brain regions with a strong ipsilateral bias.
  - Evidence 2: Further mapping of the distribution of labeled neurons in the contralateral claustrum for 63 cortical sites showed that most (50/63) cortical areas received bilateral claustral projections (Figures 4G and 4H), although the projection strength (defined by the fraction of labeled neuron [FLN]; STAR Methods) from the contralateral claustral neurons was in general much weaker than that from the ipsilateral claustral neurons (Figure 4H).

Q: What does the predominant glutamatergic subclass in the macaque claustrum correspond to in the insula?
A: The GNB4 subclass, which is predominant in macaque, predominantly corresponds to L5/6 intratelencephalic Car3 cells in the insula.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: For glutamatergic neurons, the GNB4 subclass was predominant in macaque and marmoset but was only a minority (14.7%) in mouse (Figure 2H), where ZFPM2 subclass was more abundant.
  - Evidence 2: The GLUT/GNB4 subclass was predominantly re-annotated as L5/6 intratelencephalic (IT) Car3 insular cells, with few cells mapped to L2/3 IT cells.

Q: Is the macaque claustrum more transcriptomically similar to the insula or the amygdala?
A: The claustrum is more similar to the insula; overall claustrum clusters were more similar to insular clusters, and its glutamatergic cell types were much more similar to those in the insula than in the amygdala.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Overall, claustrum clusters were more similar to insular clusters than to putamen or amygdala clusters (Figure 2B).
  - Evidence 2: Co-occurring analysis of transcriptome-based cell types between claustrum and each of three adjacent brain regions, as visualized by the heatmap of pairwise cell-type overlap scores (Figure 2C), indicated that glutamatergic cell types in the claustrum were much more similar to those in the insula than in the amygdala.

Q: How does the gene expression of the RBC compare to that of the claustrum proper?
A: The RBC highly expresses genes such as CPLX3, CABP7, and SULF1 but not claustral marker genes, and its overall gene expression pattern differs markedly from the claustrum.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: RBC highly expressed CPLX3, CABP7, and SULF1 but not the above-mentioned claustral marker genes (Figures 3A, S4A individual genes, S4B replicate data, and S4C Allen in situ hybridization [ISH] data).
  - Evidence 2: The overall RBC gene expression pattern differed markedly from that of claustrum, whereas most genes with high expression in DEn were also highly expressed in claustrum (Figure 3C).

Q: What experimental methods and marker genes were used to delineate the boundary between the RBC and the claustrum?
A: The boundary was determined using immunostaining for the RBC marker Crym and RNAscope analysis for the RBC marker gene CPLX3 and the ventral claustrum marker gene TTN.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: The boundary between RBC and claustrum was determined by immunostaining RBC marker crystallin mu (Crym)61 (Figure S4F) and RNAscope analysis of ventral claustrum marker gene TTN and RBC marker gene CPLX3 (Figure S4G).
  - Evidence 2: RBC highly expressed CPLX3, CABP7, and SULF1 but not the above-mentioned claustral marker genes (Figures 3A, S4A individual genes, S4B replicate data, and S4C Allen in situ hybridization [ISH] data).

Q: Which projection-selective zones contain neurons projecting to the hippocampal-entorhinal cortical circuit versus the motor cortical areas?
A: PSZ3 contains hippocampal-projecting and entorhinal cortical-projecting neurons, while PSZ4 contains neurons that mainly project to frontal motor areas.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Hippocampal-projecting neurons were essentially localized in PSZ3, which also harbored ENTO cortical-projecting neurons.
  - Evidence 2: PSZ4 neurons mainly projected to frontal motor areas and parietal sensory areas.

Q: What is the suggested identity of the region bordering ventromedial claustrum (RBC)?
A: The RBC is analogous to the claustrum shell or subplate in mouse but is not considered a component of the macaque claustrum.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: RBC highly expressed CPLX3, CABP7, and SULF1 but not the above-mentioned claustral marker genes (Figures 3A, S4A individual genes, S4B replicate data, and S4C Allen in situ hybridization [ISH] data).
  - Evidence 2: Therefore, we suggest that RBC is analogous to ‘‘claustrum shell’’ or ‘‘subplate’’ in mouse but that it is not a component of the macaque claustrum.

Q: Which ipsilateral cortical areas provide only weak inputs to the macaque claustrum?
A: The primary visual cortex (V1) and area 1 provide only weak inputs, as the claustrum receives strong inputs from essentially all other ipsilateral cortical areas.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: After pooling labeled neurons from all five injections, we found that claustrum received strong inputs from essentially all ipsilateral cortical areas except certain regions within the primary visual cortex (V1) and area 1 (Figure 5D), in line with previous findings.2,6,62

Q: What is the nature of the connectivity between the macaque claustrum and visual areas V1 and area 1?
A: The connectivity is largely unidirectional; the claustrum sends robust projections to V1 and area 1 but receives only weak inputs from them, suggesting a top-down influence.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Since claustral neurons had a reasonably robust projection to V1 and area 1, the connectivity between macaque claustrum and these two cortical areas is largely unidirectional, supporting the notion of a top-down claustrum influence on V1 and area 1.

Q: Does the macaque claustrum show a preference for ipsilateral or contralateral cortical inputs?
A: There is a robust ipsilateral bias, meaning cortical inputs to the macaque claustrum come predominantly from the same side of the brain.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Thus, there exists a robust ipsilateral bias of cortical inputs to the macaque claustrum.

Q: How many ipsilateral subcortical nuclei receive projections from the intra-claustrum injections, and which structures are the most strongly labeled?
A: A total of 100 ipsilateral subcortical nuclei are labeled, with particularly strong labeling in the amygdala and thalamus.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: The five intra-claustrum injections led to labeling in 100 ipsilateral subcortical nuclei, particularly strong in the amygdala and thalamus (Figure 5F; Table S7).

Q: From which subcortical structures does the C4 claustral injection site receive inputs?
A: The C4 site receives inputs exclusively from the thalamus, specifically the ventral posterior lateral nuclei (VPLo, VPLc) and the ventral posterior medial nucleus (VPM).
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Interestingly, the C4 site received projections only from the thalamus, including ventral posterior lateral nuclei (VPLo, VPLc) and ventral posterior medial nucleus (VPM) (Figures 5F and S5C), both known to be connected with the somatosensory cortex.

Q: Which ipsilateral subcortical nuclei are labeled by the C5 injection?
A: The C5 injection labels various ipsilateral subcortical nuclei, including the olfactory nucleus and the hippocampus.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: The C5 injection-labeled cells in various ipsilateral subcortical nuclei, including olfactory nucleus and the hippocampus (Figures 5F and S5C).

Q: In which projection-specific zone (PSZ) are the claustral neurons labeled by the C4 injection predominantly located?
A: They are essentially all located in PSZ4, with more than 99% of C4-labeled neurons residing there.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: In addition, by determining the percentage of labeled neurons for each injection site that was located in various cortical PSZs, we found that claustral neurons labeled by C1–C3 injections were largely (>80%) located in PSZ1, whereas those labeled by the C4 injection resided essentially (>99%) in PSZ4 (Figure 5H).

Q: Do macaque/mouse-conserved GLUT/GNB4 cell types exhibit preferential projection targets?
A: No, none of the conserved GLUT/GNB4 cell types show a target preference, whereas a substantial portion of macaque-specific ones do.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Notably, we found that none of the macaque/mouse-conserved GLUT/GNB4 cell types exhibited a target preference, while a substantial portion of the macaque-specific GLUT/GNB4 cell types exhibited a projection target preference (Figure 6B, colored in red and blue, respectively).

Q: How do the transcriptomic profiles of the claustrum, RBC, and insula compare?
A: The transcriptomic profiles of the claustrum, RBC, and insula are very different from one another.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Thus, the transcriptomic profiles were very different among claustrum, RBC, and insula.

Q: To which brain regions do RBC neurons project?
A: RBC neurons project to a restricted set of regions, including the amygdala, entorhinal cortex (ENTO), prefrontal area POLE, area 25, anterior insula, hippocampus, and superior temporal polysensory area (STPr).
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: We found that unlike claustral neurons (which projected to nearly all cortical areas), retrogradely labeled RBC neurons were found following injections in only a restricted number of brain regions, including amygdala, ENTO, POLE, area 25, anterior insula, hippocampus, and STPr (Figures S7D and examples in S7E).

Q: How does contralateral cortical input to the claustrum compare between macaques and rodents?
A: In macaques, contralateral inputs are weaker than ipsilateral ones, producing a robust ipsilateral bias, whereas in rodents contralateral inputs can be stronger than ipsilateral for some areas such as motor and cingulate cortices.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: These observations stand in sharp contrast to the findings in rodents, where contralateral inputs to the claustrum could be stronger than their homotopic ipsilateral inputs for a few cortical regions, including motor and cingulate areas.
  - Evidence 2: Thus, there exists a robust ipsilateral bias of cortical inputs to the macaque claustrum.

Q: What are the cortical and subcortical connection preferences of the C3 claustral injection site?
A: Compared to C1 and C2, the C3 site has a higher density of projections to higher visual areas and fewer to prefrontal cortex, and it receives stronger inputs from posterior thalamic nuclei such as the pulvinar, medial geniculate, and lateral geniculate, which are linked to visual processing.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Compared with C1 and C2 injections, the C3 injection resulted in a much higher density of labeled neurons in higher visual areas and fewer labeled neurons in the prefrontal cortex.
  - Evidence 2: Compared with C1 and C2 injections, the C3 injection labeled more cells in the ipsilateral posterior thalamic nuclei (Figures 5F and S5C), especially pulvinar (Puli, Pull, and Pulm), medial geniculate nucleus (MGmc and MGpc), and lateral geniculate nucleus (LGN), which are subcortical structures exhibiting widespread connectivity with visual areas and associative parietal cortex that also projected strongly to the C3 site.

Q: What are the cortical targets and subcortical inputs of the C4 claustral injection site?
A: The C4 site in the dorsal claustrum sends projections primarily to motor and somatosensory cortices, and it receives inputs solely from thalamic nuclei (VPLo, VPLc, VPM) that are connected with the somatosensory system.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Interestingly, the C4 injection site at the most dorsal extremity of the claustrum is a location that contained cells projecting selectively to motor and somatosensory cortices (Figures 4A and 4D).
  - Evidence 2: Interestingly, the C4 site received projections only from the thalamus, including ventral posterior lateral nuclei (VPLo, VPLc) and ventral posterior medial nucleus (VPM) (Figures 5F and S5C), both known to be connected with the somatosensory cortex.

Q: What are the projection targets and subcortical inputs of the C5 claustral injection site?
A: The C5 site in the middle ventral claustrum projects to ventral temporal and prefrontal cortices, including ENTO and POLE, and receives inputs from ipsilateral subcortical nuclei such as the olfactory nucleus and hippocampus.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Likewise, the C5 injection site located at the middle ventral claustrum is a location that contained cells projecting to ventral temporal and prefrontal cortices, including ENTO and POLE (Figures 4A and 4D).
  - Evidence 2: The C5 injection-labeled cells in various ipsilateral subcortical nuclei, including olfactory nucleus and the hippocampus (Figures 5F and S5C).

Q: What two key features characterize intra-claustrum connectivity in the macaque?
A: There is extensive connectivity along the rostral-to-caudal axis, but also regional specificity such that certain claustral domains avoid connections with others.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Taken together, these data indicate that there is extensive rostral-to-caudal connectivity within the claustrum, in line with the finding in rats.
  - Evidence 2: These spatial distribution patterns point to a regional specificity in intra-claustral connections.

Q: How do intra-claustrum connectivity patterns relate to the transcriptomically distinct projection-specific zones (PSZs)?
A: The connectivity patterns reflect PSZ organization: e.g., C1-C3 injections predominantly label neurons in PSZ1, while C4 labels PSZ4, and these PSZs exhibit distinct transcriptomic profiles due to different glutamatergic cell-type compositions.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: In addition, by determining the percentage of labeled neurons for each injection site that was located in various cortical PSZs, we found that claustral neurons labeled by C1–C3 injections were largely (>80%) located in PSZ1, whereas those labeled by the C4 injection resided essentially (>99%) in PSZ4 (Figure 5H).
  - Evidence 2: Thus, different PSZs exhibited distinct transcriptomic profiles due to differential compositions of glutamatergic cell types as well as varying gene expression profiles of the same cell types.

Q: Which macaque-specific cell types are enriched in the dorsal claustrum and what are their projection targets?
A: The macaque-specific GNB4 cell types GLUT/GNB4/4 and GLUT/GNB4/5 are enriched in the dorsal claustrum and project to the motor cortex and putamen, matching the motor/somatosensory projection zone defined by the C4 injection.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Interestingly, the C4 injection site at the most dorsal extremity of the claustrum is a location that contained cells projecting selectively to motor and somatosensory cortices (Figures 4A and 4D).
  - Evidence 2: Similarly, another two GNB4 cell types (GLUT/GNB4/4 and GLUT/GNB4/5) were enriched in the dorsal claustrum (containing neurons projecting to motor cortex and putamen) (Figures 6B and 7).

Q: Which macaque-specific cell types are enriched in the ventral claustrum and what are their projection targets?
A: GLUT/GNB4/2 and GLUT/GNB4/7 are enriched in the ventral claustrum and project to the entorhinal cortex and hippocampus, consistent with the C5 injection site's projection to memory-related temporal areas.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Likewise, the C5 injection site located at the middle ventral claustrum is a location that contained cells projecting to ventral temporal and prefrontal cortices, including ENTO and POLE (Figures 4A and 4D).
  - Evidence 2: Interestingly, we identified two macaque-specific GNB4 cell types (GLUT/GNB4/2 and GLUT/GNB4/7) that were enriched in ventral claustrum (containing neurons projecting to the ENTO cortex and hippocampus).
