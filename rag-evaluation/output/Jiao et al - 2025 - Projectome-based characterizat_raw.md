Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: In addition, these neurons exhibited bifurcating axon collaterals, projecting anteriorly to the GPe and posteriorly to the SNr (Fig. 4a), two crucial components of the basal ganglia circuit governing motor function[37].

  - Evidence 1: Topography along the dorso-ventral (D-V) axis was also evident, albeit in opposite orientations for projections in the SNr and GPe (Fig. 4d).

Q: Where are Penk-expressing type 4 neurons located and where do they project?

Q: What topographic correspondence is observed for Penk-expressing type 4 neurons between their soma and terminals?

Type: single-evidence
Source:

Q: To which hippocampal region do Adcyap1-expressing type 4 neurons project, and what is the axis of their topographic organization?

Q: How many axes does the topographic organization of MBO type 22 neuron projections to the ATN span?

Type: single-evidence

Q: What structural basis exists for the functional diversity observed in Orexin neurons?

Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Orexin-expressing neurons in the LHA and nearby areas, known for their complex regulatory roles in behaviors $^{46}$ , are recognized as one single transcriptionally defined type of hypothalamic excitatory neurons through scRNA-seq and multiplexed error-robust fluorescence in situ hybridization (MERFISH) analysis $^{21}$ (Extended Data Figs. 3 and 8a and Methods).
  - Evidence 2: Thus, Orexin neurons comprise multiple projectome types with distinct axon innervation patterns, providing a structural basis for the diverse functions observed in these neurons.

Q: What evidence supports that ARH Pomc neurons have a stronger projection to the midbrain compared to Agrp neurons?
A: Qualitatively, Pomc neurons display significant projections to midbrain, pons, and medulla; quantitatively, morphology-based clustering confirms a significant enrichment of the midbrain-projecting 'M' type in the Pomc population.
Type: multi-evidence

  - Evidence 2: Consistent with the finding that more Pomc neurons project to the PAG, we observed a significant enrichment $(P = 0.035)$ for 'M' type in the Pomc population (Fig. 6g).

Q: How do hypothalamic neurons co-innervate PAG subdomains, and what is an example of peptidergic-specific targeting within the newly defined subdomains?
A: Many hypothalamic PAG-projecting neurons co-innervate multiple PAG columns (e.g., pairing vlPAG with lPAG and dmPAG with dlPAG). For instance, Pomc neurons preferentially target the posterior-lateral subdomain, whereas Orexin neurons favor the posterior-medial subdomain.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Although previous studies distinguished the rostral and caudal PAG, along with a columnar organization within the caudal PAG, our research revealed many types of PAG-projecting hypothalamic neurons that co-innervated multiple PAG columns, frequently pairing the ventrolateral PAG (vlPAG) with the lateral PAG (IPAG) and the dorsomedial PAG (dmPAG) with the dorsolateral PAG (dlPAG) (Extended Data Fig. 5j-n).
  - Evidence 2: For example, ARH Pomc neurons preferentially projected to the newly identified posterior-lateral PAG subdomain (PAGpl), whereas LHA Orexin preferred the posterior-medial PAG subdomain (PAGpm) (Fig. 7d and Extended Data Fig. 10b).

Q: How does the presence or absence of topographic organization in hypothalamic peptidergic projections illustrate pathway-specificity?

  - Evidence 2: A topographic organization was notably absent in the bifurcated axon collaterals of type 22 neurons targeting the midbrain and pons (Extended Data Fig. 7b), emphasizing the pathway-specific nature of this organization.

  - Evidence 2: Notably, type 'δ' exhibited simple, unbranched axon projections, potentially extending beyond our imaging limits to the spinal cord (Fig. 5h-j), suggesting a specialized type dedicated to spinal control and sensory processing.
