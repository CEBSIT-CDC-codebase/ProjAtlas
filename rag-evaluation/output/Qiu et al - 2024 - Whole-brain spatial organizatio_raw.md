Q: What percentage of bilaterally HIP-projecting neurons originate from CA3, and how do their Schaffer collateral axons orient as they travel from CA3 to CA1?

Type: multi-evidence

Q: How does the number of multitarget projection neurons change as the number of targets increases, and what transverse topographic relationship is observed for CA1 axonal arbors?

Type: multi-evidence

  - Evidence 1: "We found that the total number of multitarget projection neurons in each of these subregions generally decreased with increasing number of downstream targets (Fig. 5B)."

Q: What is the main orientation of mossy fiber axons, and how do SUB and ProS neurons compare to CA1 and SUBr neurons in terms of target number range?

Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: "Mossy fiber axonal arbors were mainly orientated transversely in both DG and CA3 regions, with a slight longitudinal orientation in the dorsal CA3 region (Fig. 6F)."
  - Evidence 2: "In general, SUB and ProS neurons have a wider range of target number than CA1 and SUBr neurons."

Q: What percentage of all HIP neurons exhibit contralateral-only projections, and which extra-HPF target region do these rare projections primarily innervate?

  - Evidence 2: "Most of these rare contralateral projections targeted ATN (Fig. 4M), suggesting a dedicated pathway for contralateral control of this thalamic nucleus."

Q: What topographic correlation exists between CA3 soma location and Schaffer axon tips along the P-D axis, and where do CA1/SUBr neurons projecting to multiple targets tend to localize along the longitudinal axis?

Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: "We found a significant correlation of soma locations of CA3 neurons with the locations of their ipsi- and contralateral axon tips along P-D axis ( n = 765, Pearson correlation, P < 0.0001 for all; Fig. 7D)."
  - Evidence 2: "For CA1 and SUBr neurons projecting to multiple targets, their soma tended to localize ventrally (Fig. 8F)."
