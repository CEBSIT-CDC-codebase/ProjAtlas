Q: What is required for endogenous opioid antinociception according to the study?
A: Activation of mu-opioid receptors in GABAergic neurons of the central amygdala nucleus is required.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Endogenous opioid antinociception required the activation of mu-opioid receptors (MORs) in GABAergic neurons of the central amygdala nucleus (CEA) in a persistent-hyperalgesia mouse model.

Q: What is the effect of pharmacogenetic suppression of CEA^MOR neurons on persistent hyperalgesia?
A: It alleviates the persistent hyperalgesia.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Pharmacogenetic suppression of these CEAMOR neurons, which mimics the effect of MOR activation, alleviated the persistent hyperalgesia.

Q: In which brain regions is high-level MOR expression found?
A: High-level MOR expression is found in brain regions dominated by GABAergic neurons, including the striatum, nucleus accumbens, and central amygdala nucleus.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: High-level MOR expression was found in brain regions dominated by GABAergic neurons, such as the striatum, nucleus accumbens (ACB) and central amygdala nucleus (CEA) [6].

Q: What is the known role of the central amygdala nucleus in pain?
A: It is an important brain region for pain modulation.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: The CEA is known to be an important brain region for pain modulation [15].

Q: What role does the parabrachial nucleus play in chronic pain?
A: It serves as a critical relay nucleus that processes chronic pain.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Given that the PB represents the critical relay nucleus that processes chronic pain [26], this CEA-PB pathway could represent the core circuit that regulates persistent hyperalgesia via endogenous opioids.

Q: What happens to recovery from CFA-induced hyperalgesia when MORs are genetically deleted in the CEA?
A: Recovery is slowed, indicating a loss of endogenous opioid antinociception.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Thus, genetic deletion of MORs in CEA slowed recovery from CFA-induced persistent hyperalgesia, suggesting the loss of endogenous opioid antinociception.

Q: Does re-expressing MORs in the CEA affect nociceptive behaviors, locomotion, or motor coordination?
A: No, it does not significantly affect these measures.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: On the other hand, re-expression of MORs in the CEA did not significantly affect nociceptive behaviors, locomotion or motor coordination (Fig. 1P, Fig. S1G-L).

Q: What is the effect of naloxone infusion into the CEA on mechanical nociceptive threshold in mice that have recovered from CFA-induced hyperalgesia?
A: The mechanical nociceptive threshold decreases, indicating reinstatement of persistent hyperalgesia.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: We found that the mechanical nociceptive threshold decreased after the administration of naloxone (Fig. 1S), indicating the reinstatement of persistent hyperalgesia.

Q: What three stereotypical firing patterns do CEA^MOR neurons exhibit?
A: Late-firing, regular-spiking, and low-threshold bursting.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: We found that CEAMOR neurons exhibited three stereotypical firing patterns following step-depolarization: 'late-firing' neurons that began spiking ∼400 ms after current injection (Fig. S3C and D), 'regular-spiking' neurons showing steady spiking, and 'low-threshold bursting' neurons exhibiting a brief burst of spiking followed by steady spiking.

Q: How does CFA-induced persistent hyperalgesia affect the intrinsic excitability of late-firing CEA^MOR neurons?
A: It selectively increases their intrinsic excitability.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Thus, the intrinsic excitability of the 'late-firing' CEAMOR neurons was selectively increased in CFA-induced persistent hyperalgesia.

Q: What experimental evidence demonstrates that MORs in the CEA are essential for endogenous opioid antinociception?
A: Genetic deletion of MORs in the CEA impairs recovery from hyperalgesia, while re-expression of MORs in the CEA of MOR-knockout mice restores recovery.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Thus, genetic deletion of MORs in CEA slowed recovery from CFA-induced persistent hyperalgesia, suggesting the loss of endogenous opioid antinociception.
  - Evidence 2: Thus, endogenous antinociception was restored in MOR knockout mice by selective re-expression of MORs in CEA neurons, indicating the requirement of MORs in CEA for endogenous opioid antinociception.

Q: How does the excitability of CEA^MOR neurons change from the hyperalgesic state to recovery in CFA-induced persistent hyperalgesia?
A: During hyperalgesia, the intrinsic excitability of late-firing CEA^MOR neurons is increased; after recovery, their firing rate is significantly decreased.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Thus, the intrinsic excitability of the 'late-firing' CEAMOR neurons was selectively increased in CFA-induced persistent hyperalgesia.
  - Evidence 2: We found that the firing rate of CEAMOR neurons in the CFA-injected group was significantly decreased (Fig. 2R), and there was no significant difference in the rheobase (Fig. 2S) compared to that of saline-treated mice.

Q: What evidence indicates that endogenous opioids tonically suppress CEA^MOR neuron activity?
A: During hyperalgesia, late-firing CEA^MOR neurons show increased firing rates, and after recovery, blocking opioid receptors with naloxone further increases their firing rate and reduces rheobase.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Notably, we found that 'late-firing' CEAMOR neurons in CFA-treated mice exhibited a significantly increased firing rate (Fig. 2L and M) and reduced rheobase (Fig. 2N) compared to those of saline-treated mice.
  - Evidence 2: We found that the firing rate was significantly increased (Fig. 2V) and the rheobase was markedly decreased (Fig. 2W) in 'late-firing' EYFP-labeled CEAMOR neurons after naloxone treatment compared with that in saline-treated mice.

Q: What supports the idea that the CEA-PB pathway is critical for endogenous opioid antinociception?
A: The parabrachial nucleus is a critical relay for chronic pain, and suppressing CEA^MOR neuron axon branches projecting to it alleviates persistent hyperalgesia.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Given that the PB represents the critical relay nucleus that processes chronic pain [26], this CEA-PB pathway could represent the core circuit that regulates persistent hyperalgesia via endogenous opioids.
  - Evidence 2: We found that the suppression of axon branches projecting to the parabrachial nucleus (PB) of one subtype of CEAMOR neurons alleviated persistent hyperalgesia.

Q: In which subregion of the amygdala are CEA^MOR neurons functionally altered during persistent hyperalgesia?
A: Although CEA^MOR neurons are distributed across lateral, capsular, and medial subdivisions, the rheobase-reduced neurons are located predominantly in the medial subdivision (CEAm).
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: The involvement of CEAMOR neurons, which are widely distributed in the lateral (CEAl), capsular (CEAc) and medial (CEAm) subdivisions of the amygdala (Fig. S2, Movies S1 and S2), in the inflammatory hyperalgesia was further examined by measuring the activity of CEAMOR neurons with in vivo fiber photometry.
  - Evidence 2: We found that most of these rheobase-reduced neurons were located in the CEAm but not in the CEAl or CEAc (Fig. 2O).

Q: Why are CEA^MOR neurons considered important for pain modulation?
A: The central amygdala nucleus is a key pain-modulatory region, and pharmacogenetic suppression of its MOR-expressing neurons mimics MOR activation to alleviate hyperalgesia.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: The CEA is known to be an important brain region for pain modulation [15].
  - Evidence 2: Pharmacogenetic suppression of these CEAMOR neurons, which mimics the effect of MOR activation, alleviated the persistent hyperalgesia.

Q: Which subtype of CEA^MOR neurons is predominantly affected by MOR activation?
A: Late-firing neurons are the predominant subtype among CEA^MOR neurons, and MOR activation by the agonist DAMGO markedly reduces their excitability.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: 'Late-firing' neurons were predominant, and their percentages in the CEA were comparable between saline- and CFA-treated mice (Fig. 2K, Fig. S3F and G).
  - Evidence 2: Consistently, we found that the activation of MORs by bath application of the MOR agonist DAMGO during slice recording markedly reduced the excitability of these 'late-firing' CEAMOR neurons (Fig. S3T-W).

Q: What are the behavioral and electrophysiological effects of naloxone in mice that have recovered from CFA-induced hyperalgesia?
A: Naloxone infusion reinstates persistent hyperalgesia by decreasing the mechanical nociceptive threshold, and it increases the firing rate while reducing the rheobase of late-firing CEA^MOR neurons.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: We found that the mechanical nociceptive threshold decreased after the administration of naloxone (Fig. 1S), indicating the reinstatement of persistent hyperalgesia.
  - Evidence 2: We found that the firing rate was significantly increased (Fig. 2V) and the rheobase was markedly decreased (Fig. 2W) in 'late-firing' EYFP-labeled CEAMOR neurons after naloxone treatment compared with that in saline-treated mice.

Q: What is the effect of pharmacogenetic inhibition of CEA MOR neurons on CFA-induced persistent hyperalgesia?
A: It significantly decreases the persistent hyperalgesia, as indicated by increased mechanical nociceptive threshold and paw withdrawal latency.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: In CFA-treated mice, pharmacogenetic inhibition of $\mathrm{CEA}^{\mathrm{MOR}}$ neurons by intraperitoneal injection of clozapine-N-oxide (CNO, $3\mathrm{mg/kg}$ ) every other day significantly decreased persistent hyperalgesia, as indicated by the increase in the mechanical nociceptive threshold and paw withdrawal latency (Fig. 3C and D, Fig. S4F and G), but there were no effects on the locomotion or motor coordination of the mice (Fig. S4H and I).

Q: What effect did prolonged activation of CEA MOR neurons have on CFA-induced persistent hyperalgesia?
A: It aggravated the hyperalgesia without significantly affecting locomotion.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: We found that prolonged activation of $\mathrm{CEA}^{\mathrm{MOR}}$ neurons aggravated CFA-induced persistent hyperalgesia without significantly affecting locomotion (Fig. 3G-I, Fig. S4P and Q).

Q: How were CEA MOR neurons ablated in the study?
A: They were ablated by bilaterally injecting AAV-CAG-DIO-taCasp3-TEVp virus, a caspase3-based method, into the CEA.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: We bilaterally injected AAV-CAG-DIO-taCasp3-TEVp virus, or AAV-Ef1 α -DIO-EYFP virus as a control, into the CEA of MOR-iCreERT $^2$ ×Ai9 mice

Q: How did ablation of CEA MOR neurons affect recovery from CFA-induced persistent hyperalgesia?
A: Recovery was faster in CEA MOR-taCasp3 mice than in control mice.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: By measuring the thermal nociceptive latency and mechanical nociceptive threshold, we found that the recovery of CFA-induced persistent hyperalgesia was faster in $\mathrm{CEA}^{\mathrm{MOR}}$ -taCasp3 mice than in control mice (Fig. 3M and N).

Q: How many individual CEA MOR neurons were reconstructed in the single-neuron projectome analysis?
A: 67 individual neurons were reconstructed.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: The brain-wide axon projections of 67 individual $\mathrm{CEA}^{\mathrm{MOR}}$ neurons were reconstructed by a semiautomatic tracing method

Q: Which four brain regions receive strong projections from CEA MOR neurons?
A: The midbrain reticular nucleus (MRN), lateral hypothalamic area (LHA), PB, and bed nuclei of the stria terminalis (BST).
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: We found strong projections of $\mathrm{CEA}^{\mathrm{MOR}}$ neurons to the midbrain reticular nucleus (MRN), lateral hypothalamic area (LHA), PB and bed nuclei of the stria terminalis (BST)

Q: What are the primary projection targets of subtype-1 CEA MOR neurons?
A: They project primarily to mid- and hindbrain areas, including the PB, MRN, and periaqueductal gray (PAG).
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Subtype-1 $\mathrm{CEA}^{\mathrm{MOR}}$ neurons exhibited the most extensive axons with primarily posterior long-range projections to mid- and hindbrain areas, including the PB, MRN and periaqueductal gray (PAG)

Q: What are the main projection targets of subtype-2 CEA MOR neurons?
A: They primarily project to the BST and substantia innominate (SI).
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Subtype-2 neurons primarily projected to the BST and substantia innominate (SI), whereas subtype-3 and subtype-4 $\mathrm{CEA}^{\mathrm{MOR}}$ neurons projected to forebrain regions such as the cortical subplate (CTXsp), striatum (STR) and pallidum (PAL)

Q: In which CEA subregions are the somata of subtype-1 CEA MOR neurons clustered?
A: They are clustered in the CEAl and CEAm subregions.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: The somata of subtype-1 were clustered in the CEAl and CEAm.

Q: What neurotransmitter phenotype are CEA MOR neurons known to have?
A: They are GABAergic.
Type: single-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: These CEA neurons are known to be GABAergic [6]; thus, this result is consistent with previous studies showing that MORs in GABAergic neurons are involved in endogenous opioid analgesia [6,7].

Q: What evidence indicates that CEA MOR neuron activity gates CFA-induced persistent hyperalgesia?
A: Pharmacogenetic inhibition of CEA MOR neurons decreases hyperalgesia, and ablation of these neurons accelerates recovery from hyperalgesia.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: In CFA-treated mice, pharmacogenetic inhibition of $\mathrm{CEA}^{\mathrm{MOR}}$ neurons by intraperitoneal injection of clozapine-N-oxide (CNO, $3\mathrm{mg/kg}$ ) every other day significantly decreased persistent hyperalgesia, as indicated by the increase in the mechanical nociceptive threshold and paw withdrawal latency (Fig. 3C and D, Fig. S4F and G), but there were no effects on the locomotion or motor coordination of the mice (Fig. S4H and I).
  - Evidence 2: By measuring the thermal nociceptive latency and mechanical nociceptive threshold, we found that the recovery of CFA-induced persistent hyperalgesia was faster in $\mathrm{CEA}^{\mathrm{MOR}}$ -taCasp3 mice than in control mice (Fig. 3M and N).

Q: How was the CEA-to-PB pathway shown to mediate antinociception?
A: Both pharmacogenetic and optogenetic suppression of CEA MOR neuron terminals in the PB alleviated CFA-induced persistent hyperalgesia.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: We found that CFA-induced persistent hyperalgesia was alleviated by local ipsilateral CNO infusion in hM4Di-expressing mice, in which ipsilateral projections from $\mathrm{CEA}^{\mathrm{MOR}}$ neurons to PB were suppressed, and the same CNO infusion in EYFP-expressing control mice had no effect (Fig. 5C and D).
  - Evidence 2: We found that optogenetic suppression of bilateral or ipsilateral but not contralateral projections from subtype-1 $\mathrm{CEA}^{\mathrm{MOR}}$ neurons to the PB markedly alleviated CFA-induced persistent hyperalgesia (Fig. 5H–J), without affecting basal nociceptive sensitivity or locomotion (Fig. S7L–O).

Q: How do the projection patterns of subtype-1 and subtype-2 CEA MOR neurons relate to their roles in pain modulation?
A: Subtype-1 neurons project to mid/hindbrain areas involved in pain modulation, while suppressing the BST projection of subtype-2 neurons has no effect on hyperalgesia.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Subtype-1 $\mathrm{CEA}^{\mathrm{MOR}}$ neurons exhibited the most extensive axons with primarily posterior long-range projections to mid- and hindbrain areas, including the PB, MRN and periaqueductal gray (PAG)
  - Evidence 2: We found that optogenetic suppression of $\mathrm{CEA}^{\mathrm{MOR}}$ axon projections in the BST, which represents a major target of subtype-2 neurons (Fig. 4F and G, and Fig. S6M), had no effect on CFA-induced persistent hyperalgesia and locomotion (Fig. 5U and V, and Fig. S7V-X), indicating the projection subtype specificity of $\mathrm{CEA}^{\mathrm{MOR}}$ neurons in the endogenous modulation of nociception.

Q: What is the effect of optogenetically suppressing CEA MOR neuron projections to the PAG?
A: It induces hyperalgesia, significantly decreasing mechanical nociceptive threshold and paw withdrawal latency.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Subtype-1 $\mathrm{CEA}^{\mathrm{MOR}}$ neurons exhibited the most extensive axons with primarily posterior long-range projections to mid- and hindbrain areas, including the PB, MRN and periaqueductal gray (PAG)
  - Evidence 2: We found that suppression of the $\mathrm{CEA}^{\mathrm{MOR}}$ -PAG projection induced a significant decrease in the mechanical nociceptive threshold and paw withdrawal latency (Fig. 5M and N), indicating that this suppression induced hyperalgesia.

Q: How do CEA MOR neurons affect PB neuron activity?
A: They provide inhibitory inputs, consistent with their known GABAergic phenotype.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Using whole-cell recording of brain slices, we confirmed that $\mathrm{CEA}^{\mathrm{MOR}}$ neurons provided inhibitory inputs to PB neurons (Fig. S7A–G), which was consistent with previous reports [38,39].
  - Evidence 2: These CEA neurons are known to be GABAergic [6]; thus, this result is consistent with previous studies showing that MORs in GABAergic neurons are involved in endogenous opioid analgesia [6,7].

Q: What evidence shows that PB-projecting CEA MOR neurons are distinct from PKC-delta positive neurons in the CEA?
A: Subtype-1 CEA MOR neurons project to the PB, and these PB-projecting neurons are known to be distinct from PKC-delta positive neurons in the CEA.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: Subtype-1 $\mathrm{CEA}^{\mathrm{MOR}}$ neurons exhibited the most extensive axons with primarily posterior long-range projections to mid- and hindbrain areas, including the PB, MRN and periaqueductal gray (PAG)
  - Evidence 2: Thus, PB-projecting $\mathrm{CEA}^{\mathrm{MOR}}$ neurons are distinct from PKC $\delta^{+}$ neurons in the CEA (Fig. S8E-G).

Q: How many subtypes of CEA MOR neurons were identified, and what method was used for classification?
A: Four subtypes were identified using hierarchical clustering with Euclidean distance and Ward's linkages, after reconstructing 67 individual neurons.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: The brain-wide axon projections of 67 individual $\mathrm{CEA}^{\mathrm{MOR}}$ neurons were reconstructed by a semiautomatic tracing method
  - Evidence 2: Four different subtypes of $\mathrm{CEA}^{\mathrm{MOR}}$ neurons were identified based on their projections by hierarchical clustering using Euclidean distance metric and Ward's linkages (Fig. 4E-H and Fig. S6C), excluding subtypes with fewer than 10 reconstructed neurons.

Q: Which CEA MOR neuron projections were found not to affect CFA-induced persistent hyperalgesia?
A: Projections to the BST and VTA did not significantly affect hyperalgesia or locomotion.
Type: multi-evidence
Source:
  - Evidence 1: We found that optogenetic suppression of $\mathrm{CEA}^{\mathrm{MOR}}$ axon projections in the BST, which represents a major target of subtype-2 neurons (Fig. 4F and G, and Fig. S6M), had no effect on CFA-induced persistent hyperalgesia and locomotion (Fig. 5U and V, and Fig. S7V-X), indicating the projection subtype specificity of $\mathrm{CEA}^{\mathrm{MOR}}$ neurons in the endogenous modulation of nociception.
  - Evidence 2: We next examined the effect of optogenetically suppressing subtype-1 $\mathrm{CEA}^{\mathrm{MOR}}$ neuron projections in the VTA (Fig. 5O and P), and found that this suppression did not significantly affect the nociceptive threshold during CFA-induced persistent hyperalgesia and locomotion (Fig. 5Q and R, and Fig. S7S-U).

Q: What evidence indicates that endogenous opioids in the CEA suppress CEAMOR neuron activity to produce antinociception?

Type: multi-evidence
Source:

  - Evidence 2: "The notion that the CFA-induced hyperalgesia results from an increase of neuronal excitability of CEAMOR neurons, and that endogenous opioids suppress this increase, was further supported by our electrophysiological recording results, which show that naloxone elevated firing of CEAMOR neurons at day 21 after CFA injection when the mice had recovered from persistent hyperalgesia (Fig. 2T-W)."

Q: How was the role of subtype-1 CEAMOR neurons in endogenous opioid antinociception demonstrated?

  - Evidence 2: "We demonstrated that subtype-1 CEAMOR neurons were engaged in endogenous opioid antinociception via their axon projections to the PB, which is widely known to be an important central relay for nociceptive information processing [37,68]."
